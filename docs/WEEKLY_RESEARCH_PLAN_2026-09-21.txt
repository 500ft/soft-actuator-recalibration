Purpose: test whether the observed C failures are concentrated in units with

Files to add:

The analysis must consume the committed Study C result and the same deterministic
unit generator. It must not refit the ridge model, change lambda, alter the
held-out split, or select a new threshold.

For each of the 10 held-out units, compute and store:

- stable unit identity and the unit's rupture_cycles;
- acceleration_onset_fraction;
- number of probes and cycle values;
- number and fraction of probes before onset;
- number and fraction of probes at or after onset;

- normalized life at the last observed probe;

analysis document before calculating it. Recommended initial rule: sparse means

study.

Aggregate outputs must include:

R2 — FREEZE STUDY C2: INFORMATION-TIMING AND SCHEDULE SENSITIVITY

Owner: Claude executor prepares the document; owner reviews before execution.
Type: new preregistered simulation design. Estimate: 3–4 hours.

File to add before any C2 run:

This is a follow-up to diagnose C-FAIL, not an amendment that changes C's verdict.
The document must contain the question, hypotheses, schedule grid, random seeds,
metrics, split, selection rules, stopping rules, and downgrade language before
R3 or R4 begins. Record its commit hash in the evidence file.

Primary question:

Does giving the same estimator more observations in the informative regime reduce
the held-out failure pattern, or does Study B's post-onset non-identifiability
remain the limiting factor?

Hypotheses:

- H-C2-SCHEDULE: fixed-cycle schedules with better post-onset coverage reduce
  the short-lived-unit error and improve the C metrics without changing features
  or estimator architecture.
- H-C2-SIGNAL: even a diagnostic schedule with post-onset coverage does not reach
  the target; pressure features remain confounded with onset/leak nuisance state.
- H-C2-MIXED: the schedule helps some units but cannot meet the cohort threshold
  under a practical probe budget.

Fixed cohort and split:

- reuse sample_units(N=30, seed=SEED) and the exact seeded train/test permutation
  from scripts/run_studyC.py;
- 20 train and 10 held-out units by identity;
- no trace-level random split;
- no new units or new degradation-family claim;
- keep Study C's 10 held-out identities unchanged;
- do not use Study C held-out outcomes to choose a schedule, lambda, features, or
  any other hyperparameter.

Estimator contract:

- exactly the current C estimator: ridge regression;
- exactly the current 11-input representation: five log ratios to the young
  baseline, five previous-probe log ratios, and cycle count / 1000;
- same three lambdas (0.01, 0.1, 1.0), selected by leave-one-unit-out on the
  20 training units separately for each schedule;
- standardization fitted on training units and reused unchanged on held-out units;
- no new estimator variants, neural networks, feature selection, or post-hoc
  threshold tuning.

Schedule contract:

- every condition obtains a baseline at cycle 100;
- follow-up starts are 100, 250, or 500 cycles;
- follow-up cadences are 125, 250, or 500 cycles;
- probes stop before rupture;
- duplicate cycle-100 baseline observations are de-duplicated;
- include the original “start 100, cadence 250” condition as a named reference;
- keep the original Study C seed namespace for that reference condition so its
  outputs can be compared exactly to the committed run;
- use a separate, deterministic namespace for new schedules, such as
  SeedSequence([SEED, 8, unit_index, schedule_id, probe_index]);
- report mean, median, minimum, and maximum probe count per condition;
- do not report a schedule as practical without its probe count and coverage cost.

Diagnostic oracle arm:

- baseline remains at cycle 100;
- add a predeclared diagnostic schedule that places probes at the unit's true
  onset and at two fixed post-onset cycle offsets when those probes remain before
  rupture;
- the oracle uses generator truth and is impossible to deploy; label it
  “mechanistic diagnostic upper bound,” never a production policy;
- fit and evaluate the same estimator contract under this schedule;
- use it only to distinguish observation timing from feature identifiability.

If an oracle schedule is considered too strong, retain it anyway as a diagnostic
arm or document the explicit decision to omit it before implementation. Do not
quietly replace it with a schedule chosen after inspecting C outcomes.

Metrics for every schedule, on every held-out unit:

- u-RMSE;
- RMSE before and after onset;
- clock-only RMSE using the training-median rupture life;
- delta versus clock;
- number and fraction of post-onset probes;
- probe count and probe-count ratio versus the original schedule;
- target pass (RMSE <= 0.10);
- beat-clock flag;
- C-PASS counts using the unchanged rule: at least 8/10 target passes and at
  least 8/10 units better than clock.

Uncertainty and reporting:

- actuator-cluster bootstrap over complete held-out units with the existing 2000
  resamples and a recorded seed;
- leave-one-held-out-unit-out range;
- retain all per-unit rows, including failures and missing pre/post segments;
- report every schedule row, not only the best row;
- do not compute a p-value or claim statistical significance from n=10.

Predeclared interpretation rules:

- schedule-limited evidence: the oracle and at least one feasible fixed schedule
  materially reduce the short-lived-unit errors, and a feasible schedule reaches
  the unchanged C-PASS rule;
- signal-limited evidence: the oracle does not reach the target or does not
  remove the post-onset error pattern, even with adequate coverage;
- mixed evidence: the oracle helps, but no feasible schedule meets C-PASS under
  the stated probe budget;
- null/clock-dominated evidence: most schedules do not beat the clock and any
  gains occur only where the clock is already inaccurate; no transfer claim.

“Materially reduce” must be fixed in the preregistration as a numeric descriptive
rule before the run. Recommended rule: at least 0.05 life reduction in mean RMSE
on the three currently worst short-lived held-out units, with the same estimator
and a stated probe-count cost. If the owner chooses another rule, record it before
R3.
