MINIMUM ACCEPTANCE WORKFLOW FOR A FUTURE PART

1. Resolve inputs. Keep cad/pilot/parameters.csv as the proposed evidence register.
   For every required value, retain units, source, evidence state and acquisition
   route. Distinguish design_then_inspect, vendor_drawing and measurement. A vendor
   drawing cannot close a measurement requirement. Unresolved required dimensions
   block that part; optional unresolved features may be omitted only with the
   reduced scope explicitly recorded.

2. Create one explicit geometry contract for the part, supplied to both the
   authoring route and its independent geometry checker. If derived from the CSV,
   record the source rows/revision and the conversion; do not maintain conflicting
   dimensions in two places. Tag designer choices provisional_design and preserve
   their inspection requirement.

3. Define expected geometry before authoring. Use an independent hand calculation
   or separately implemented CadQuery construction. Sharing input dimensions is
   useful; sharing the candidate's computed output as its expected answer is not.
   Declare absolute/relative tolerances and units in advance. The briefing's
   near-machine-precision agreements are observations, not universal tolerances.

4. Check the quantities that matter to this part. Volume is necessary where used
   as the oracle, but it does not establish hole position, wall thickness, chamber
   connectivity or fit. Add bounding box, body count, bore/port location, minimum
   wall bounds and clearance checks as the part's contract requires. Include a
   deliberately wrong feature case that the relevant check must reject.

5. Author and instrument. Verify sketch orientation with a small measured probe;
   do not assume another host's plane mapping. Record per-feature measurements,
   rejected equations and null feature returns. Check saved files exist and are
   non-empty. A clean rebuild or plausible screenshot is insufficient evidence.

6. Prove parameter control. Change a declared parameter within a reviewed range,
   rebuild, and compare against independently computed new expectations. Include
   position/clearance effects where relevant. Equations appearing in a feature
   tree do not establish that all dependent features actually move.

7. Export STEP and re-import in a fresh process. Recheck the geometry contract,
   retain source/export hashes and tool versions, and record a clear verdict.
   If no independent expected values exist, report accepted: null / unverified.

8. Deliver editable geometry, neutral export, numeric evidence and explanatory
   views. Dimensions, fit tolerances, materials and inspection requirements must
   still be supplied for fabrication review. Poll actual host completion and
   retain timeout/failure evidence; a successful launch is not a completed build.

FEA LIMITS TO CARRY FORWARD

The briefing's initial solve is a rigid plate example. It explicitly states that
the FEA side has no independent oracle. Before interpreting fixture stress or
displacement, plan an independent benchmark, boundary/load checks, units checks
and mesh-convergence evidence for the intended problem.

It supplies no demonstrated validation of this project's elastomer deformation,
fatigue, recovery or pneumatic coupling. A successful plate solve cannot close
those research questions. Formulating and validating such a model would be a
separate research task if the project needs it.

Native assembly authoring, mates, drawing automation and fully parametric feature
positions are gaps named by the briefing. Recheck their evidence when the tooling
is next used; do not promise them from this snapshot.
