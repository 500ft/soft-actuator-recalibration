RECOMMENDATION

Keep the research direction, but repair the inference chain before adding a more
complex estimator. The strongest next question is: under which conditions does
measured probe information improve a recalibration decision over a clock, after
accounting for uncertainty and acquisition cost?

There are concrete issues in the current Study B follow-up and C2 design. These
are more urgent than another broad literature search. The proposed changes below
are prospective work packages for Claude, not instructions to silently rewrite
preregistered studies or their outputs.

1. REOPEN THE STUDY B FOLLOW-UP'S RESOLUTION CLAIM — PRIORITY P0

Evidence from the repository:
- scripts/run_studyB_structural.py:76-82 stacks the young baseline and current
  snapshot for the collinearity calculation.
- Its likelihood section at lines 106-118 instead constructs one five-feature
  observation and one covariance, then calls profile_likelihood_u.
- pipeline/identifiability.py:172-203 evaluates that single snapshot. It does not
  include a young-baseline residual. The output metadata nevertheless describes
  the design as B2, snapshot plus young baseline.
- U_GRID ends at 0.90, which is also the post-onset truth. Stored points 0.80,
  0.85, and 0.90 have almost identical likelihood values. There is no tested point
  above 0.90 to locate the upper confidence crossing.
- profile_verdict classifies any profile staying below a statistical threshold
  across the grid as structural. A shallow noisy profile over a finite interval
  does not establish an exact structural symmetry.

Literature basis: Raue et al. [S1] define profiling by nuisance re-optimization
and distinguish a flat structural direction from limited statistical information.
Wieland et al. [S2] explain why Fisher-based uncertainty alone is inadequate.
Neither licenses turning an arbitrary grid edge into a confidence bound.

Assessment: high confidence in the code/report mismatch and the missing upper
crossing. The correct structural-versus-practical conclusion remains unresolved
by this review. The earlier Fisher evidence still supports a difficult estimation
region under its assumptions; it does not establish the new +/-0.05 resolution.

Proposed repair:
1. Explicitly separate B1 snapshot and B2 baseline-plus-snapshot likelihoods.
   For B2, share the nuisance parameters between both observations and use the
   appropriate joint covariance. Retain B1 as a labeled comparison.
2. Match B2's declared known baseline coordinate for the diagnostic replication.
   Separately disclose that u_base=0.05 requires total-life knowledge. A practical
   successor must use the known baseline cycle and treat lifetime as unknown.
3. Scale positive nuisance parameters, use multiple starts and continuation, and
   store fitted nuisance values, bound hits, objective values, and convergence.
   The current result has one nonconverged pre-onset grid point; the classifier
   does not inspect convergence. Do not interpret unresolved solver failures.
4. Extend the grid on both sides within the declared physical domain and refine
   near crossings. Report a domain-limited/open bound when no crossing is found.
   Derive the reference minimum from a verified joint fit, not just a coarse grid.
5. Use a noiseless symmetry/identifiability analysis to investigate structural
   ambiguity. Use noisy profiles for practical uncertainty. A noise sweep is
   informative but is not by itself a global structural proof.
6. Treat chi-square thresholds as an approximation whose adequacy needs checking
   for this nonlinear, bounded, finite-data problem. Use predeclared parametric
   coverage checks if confidence-interval claims are retained.

Acceptance: a dimension check proves B2 uses all ten observations; a baseline
perturbation changes the likelihood; failed optimization produces unresolved
status; an open interval is never printed as +/-0.05; a weak but identifiable toy
model cannot be called structurally non-identifiable merely for staying below a
threshold. Preserve the original output and issue a separately versioned correction.

2. STATE THE ACTUAL SENSING REQUIREMENTS — PRIORITY P0

Evidence: pipeline/identifiability.py:18-64 computes two P-V loop areas, a secant
stiffness with volume in its denominator, and two pressure-decay features. It
explicitly measures noisy volume. C2's pressure_only arm removes the cycle column;
it retains all the volume-dependent features and their lags.

Assessment: this establishes an arm without an explicit clock, not sensing from
pressure alone. This is distinct from the repository's legitimate separation
between pressure-only pose estimation and intermittent P-V health probes.

Proposed change:
- Describe the existing arm as "probe features without cycle count" in reporting,
  with an explicit mapping to the legacy pressure_only identifier.
- Add a channel contract: measured pressure, measured/estimated volume, commanded
  excitation, baseline data, elapsed time, and unavailable generator truth.
- If the intended system has no volume sensor, specify and validate the volume
  proxy. Pump stroke is not automatically chamber volume in a pneumatic system.
- A genuinely pressure-measurement-only decay arm is a separately preregistered
  comparison; it answers a different question from deleting the clock column.
- Account for all acquisitions. unit_records measures a normalization baseline
  and another observation at cycle 100; the current row count is not automatically
  the total acquisition count or cost.

Literature boundary: Wall et al. [S7] concern acoustic sensing; Bui [S5] measures
trajectory hysteresis; Torzini [S6] monitors bending during fatigue. Those results
cannot establish a pressure-only fatigue estimator or a P-V sensing budget.

Acceptance: every feature has an observable input and acquisition route; public
claim wording matches that contract; no volume-derived quantity is silently
classified as requiring only pressure. Apply this contract to future CAD/rig work
using CAD_PLANNING_ADDENDUM_2026-09-21.txt, without starting hardware now.

3. MAKE SCHEDULE COMPARISONS MEASURE THE SAME OUTCOME — PRIORITY P0

Evidence: run_studyC2.py:evaluate_cell evaluates each schedule on that schedule's
own probe rows. The oracle moves most observations into post-onset life, so it
also moves most scored targets there. Matched probe count does not match the
evaluation distribution. Changes in score mix estimator changes with changes in
which states are being scored.

The oracle also uses actual onset, rupture time, and a lifetime-derived probe
count. It is a privileged-information schedule, not a proven upper bound on
achievable performance. Ridge fitting need not improve monotonically when data
are moved. An oracle failure cannot demonstrate that every feasible schedule fails.

Assessment: high confidence in the mismatch; unknown magnitude or direction until
a separately specified comparison is run. This is a design inference from the
code. The model-evaluation caution in [S3] and the experimental-design reasoning
in [S1] motivate keeping the target question explicit.

Proposed successor protocol:
1. Freeze a common evaluation clock before generating outcomes, such as an
   absolute-cycle grid at a declared spacing from baseline until failure. Use
   identical evaluation times for every schedule on each unit. Report which
   units survive each time and aggregate within unit before across units.
2. At each time, use only observations already acquired. Choose the between-probe
   prediction rule in advance: hold-last-state or a trained causal propagation
   rule. A prediction at an unprobed time cannot consume a hidden new probe.
3. Keep probe-time RMSE as a secondary endpoint. Make time-weighted whole-life
   error and a declared early-operation window explicit. Normalized-life bins
   are acceptable for offline analysis, never for choosing online actions.
4. Report acquisition count, probe duration, and time without a new measurement.
   The existing dense arm changes both count and timing; describe it as a budget
   comparison. Add a feasible placement comparison at a predeclared common budget
   and horizon only if placement itself is the causal question.
5. Use the same baseline observation across schedules in a paired comparison.
   Current seeds give the same baseline state but different baseline noise.
   Predeclare shared-time noise pairing or independent measurement replicates;
   never search seeds for an attractive outcome.
6. Keep the historical reference cell intact. A new primary estimand requires a
   versioned protocol, not silently replacing the old plotted number.

Two secondary controls are worth recording. The lag refers to different elapsed
times at different cadences. Also, ridge_fit uses an unnormalized summed loss,
so the same lambda has a different effective penalty as the number of rows changes.
Long-lived units supply more training rows. For a successor, choose unit weighting
and mean-loss regularization explicitly and report elapsed time since last probe.
These are controlled design changes, not evidence that a particular remedy works.

Acceptance: every schedule is scored on the same per-unit timestamps; causal
replay cannot read future observations, rupture, or onset; baseline pairing is
verified; there is no claim that the oracle supplies a mathematical performance bound.

4. REPLACE OVERSTRONG INTERPRETATION LABELS — PRIORITY P0

Evidence: C2 defines "indistinguishable" by whether the clock-only mean lies in
full's leave-one-unit-out range. The runner implements that condition directly.
This range is a sensitivity diagnostic, not a confidence interval for a paired
model difference and not an equivalence test. The final fallback label is
signal-limited even when other explanations remain untested.

Literature basis: equivalence requires a justified margin and an appropriate test
or interval procedure, rather than failure to see a difference [S4]. An estimator
failure is not an identifiability theorem [S1, S2].

Proposed change:
- Preserve per-unit paired differences d_i = error(full)_i - error(clock)_i.
  Report their distribution and a whole-unit paired uncertainty interval.
- For an equivalence claim, preregister a smallest meaningful difference and a
  paired equivalence procedure with its assumptions. At ten units, "inconclusive"
  may be the correct result. Do not invent the margin after viewing the interval.
- Name the two baselines separately: median-life clock and learned clock-only
  ridge. Beating the former does not establish a gain over the latter.
- Replace causal conclusions with descriptive outcomes such as "full arm improves
  under schedule X" and "no tested arm met the historical criterion."
- Require additional evidence before saying clock-prior-limited, schedule-limited,
  or signal-limited. Report competing explanations: feature construction, model
  misspecification, training weights, noise, representation, and sample uncertainty.

Acceptance: an unresolved comparison stays unresolved; an always-failing ridge
grid cannot emit "features cannot carry life"; a practical schedule's performance
is displayed even when the reference clock comparison meets another label rule.

5. SEPARATE DIAGNOSIS FROM CONFIRMATION — PRIORITY P1

Evidence: C2 reuses the ten Study C test identities after their failures informed
the study design. The frozen subset happens to be the previously identified three
short-lived failures. A generator-defined subset rule improves transparency, but
it does not make this a fresh independent test.

Literature basis: evaluation-informed selection can overfit a finite sample even
when coefficient fitting itself is clean [S3]. Wall's transfer experiment [S7]
supports retaining identity-level separation, but does not quantify this project's
generalization error or prescribe its pass threshold.

Proposed evaluation layers:
A. Historical reproduction: preserve Study C and the original C2 diagnostic grid.
B. Development: use training-only unit folds to choose the prospective arm,
   schedule, regularization, weights, and any rejection threshold.
C. Confirmation: freeze those decisions, then generate fresh independent cohorts
   under an explicit new study identifier. No best-seed selection or retuning on C.

Distinguish repeated splits of the same 30 units from fresh cohorts: the former
measures split sensitivity, not uncertainty over new populations. If training
randomness is part of the claim, replicate the whole train/select/test procedure.
Do not count folds, time rows, or overlapping resplits as independent actuators.

Choose replication count from precision and measured runtime. As a planning
reference only, a Bernoulli rate near 0.8 needs roughly 246 independent outcomes
for a normal-approximation 95% half-width of 0.05; the worst-case reference is
385. These are not a hardware sample-size mandate, an exact interval calculation,
or a substitute for accounting for shared training and cohort dependence.

Keep the historical 8/10 rule. Also show the two continuous objectives separately:
absolute accuracy and paired incremental value over the chosen clock. Do not
lower the pass bar to rescue a result. Three tested CV values do not prove no
dispersion value or no estimator could ever satisfy both conditions.

Acceptance: each new result identifies the population, split, selection procedure,
seed manifest, and uncertainty level; no confirmatory claim rests on selecting the
best of the 15 original cells using the same ten known units.

6. TEST GENERATOR MISMATCH, WITHOUT REPLACING ONE UNSUPPORTED DEFAULT WITH ANOTHER
   — PRIORITY P1

The dispersion audit is useful, but its "measured-realistic" wording should remain
conditional on the particular published designs. Small cohorts from specific
materials, geometry, and loading protocols do not establish a universal lifetime
CV. Keep 0.30 labeled as an assumed stress case; retain lower-dispersion scenarios
without claiming either is the measured distribution of our future apparatus [S6].

Bui reports an early break-in region and later less systematic trajectory changes;
its endpoint is not P-V area and its endurance test does not identify this project's
full fatigue-to-failure law [S5]. The current model's Mullins time constant is three
cycles and the baseline occurs at cycle 100. A slower transient could contaminate
that baseline. This is a plausible confound to test, not proof the generator is wrong.

Proposed small challenge suite, one change at a time before combinations:
- Existing generator, unchanged, as the reproduction case.
- Slower saturating break-in, with a predeclared range and distinct latent break-in
  and irreversible-damage states. Never impose a universal 160-cycle constant.
- Variable rest and within-unit thermal/rate changes at a fixed damage state.
- An alternative failure definition with an observable endpoint, alongside the
  existing imposed rupture-life model; keep their inference claims separate.

For the probe-rate question, vary frequency, amplitude, rest, and temperature while
holding damage fixed, then vary damage with those conditions fixed. Compare feature
and decision changes. Existing 1 and 4 Hz features are a starting point, not enough
to establish a quasi-static limit. A dynamic dissipation signal can still be useful;
rate dependence does not automatically invalidate it [S8].

Avoid an overstrong literature rebuttal: Severson's early-life battery prediction
shows that informative precursors can exist before capacity fade [S9]. It does not
disprove a scheduling mechanism in this soft-actuator generator. Its value is as a
reason to test early features rather than assume late probes must be better.

Acceptance: scenarios have declared assumptions and supported ranges where available;
every feature is challenged against nondamage perturbations; same-family validation
and new-mechanism validation are reported separately; no synthetic result is called
a physical calibration.

7. CONNECT THE TARGET TO RECALIBRATION — PRIORITY P1, AFTER THE EVALUATION REPAIR

The current u = cycles/rupture_cycles is a retrospective life coordinate, not a
directly measured material state. The September 23 audit confirms that the model
allows different leak states at rupture. Equal u need not mean equal pose error,
equal remaining usable performance, or equal benefit from recalibration.

Proposed decision study:
- Define a task-space pose-error budget in physical units and a future decision
  horizon. Distinguish terminal rupture, functional failure, and recalibration.
- Predict either current calibration error or the risk of exceeding the budget
  within that horizon. Retain u prediction as a secondary mechanistic endpoint.
- Compare fixed schedules, the frozen state-based policy, and a declared reference
  with extra sensing on the same units and operating trajectories.
- Report time over the error budget, missed crossings, calibration count, probe
  count, and unavailable operating time. Use a Pareto plot until acquisition and
  downtime costs have defensible values; do not manufacture cost savings.
- Calibrating the pose map may reduce mapping error, but must not reset simulated
  material damage or lifetime. Explicitly model what information recalibration buys.

de Jonge et al. [S10] show in maintenance simulations why measurement quality,
failure uncertainty, and operational costs can change the value of condition-based
decisions. Application to recalibration here is our proposed adaptation, not an
established result for soft robots.

Future pilot requirement: obtain synchronized pressure, a justified volume measure,
and a camera/reference pose, with rest, temperature, specimen identity, loading, and
acquisition time logged. One reused specimen can establish feasibility and challenge
confounds; it cannot establish unseen-unit transfer. Any few-shot calibration must
use labels obtainable during operation. The current per-unit u-label oracle needs
future rupture knowledge and cannot serve as a deployable calibration procedure.

Acceptance: the primary score answers whether the robot should recalibrate, the
clock baseline has the same operational constraints, and sensing/downtime costs
are visible even when only reported parametrically.

8. BEST NEXT MODEL IDEA: LET MEASUREMENTS EARN THEIR CORRECTION — PRIORITY P2

This is a proposed hypothesis, not a literature-certified fix. The dispersion audit
suggests pressure-derived information helps on some units while degrading a good
clock estimate on others. Start with a cheap nested correction before a large model:

  prediction = clock_prediction + alpha * residual_model(probe_history)

Fit the residual model on training units; select alpha, including alpha=0, using
unit-level validation. Compute baseline parameters separately inside each training
fold so that validation labels do not enter the baseline. Use mean loss and explicit
unit weights. Compare this to both named clock baselines and the current full ridge.

Begin with one global alpha. A per-unit gate is justified only after held-out
development evidence shows it predicts harmful corrections from currently available
information. True lifetime, retrospective error, and the oracle onset cannot drive
that gate. Evaluate risk versus retained coverage and report the fallback policy's
error/cost on every rejected case; discarding difficult units is not improvement.

If this simple model is inadequate, preregister a hierarchical population model
with updates from each unit's observed trajectory. Empirical-Bayes degradation work
provides a precedent [S11], not a guarantee that pressure contains enough information.
More flexible models cannot recover an unobservable state by architecture alone.

Novelty boundary: selective prediction already exists [S12]. Do not describe an
abstain mechanism as new in general. A potentially distinct contribution is its
validated use to choose pressure-informed recalibration versus a specified fallback
under actuator variability and acquisition cost. That novelty still needs checking.

Acceptance: alpha=0 reproduces the chosen clock; all thresholds are fitted without
confirmation labels; effects are reported on fresh cohorts; improvement is measured
including fallback cases and across the declared dispersion scenarios.

EVIDENCE-STRENGTH SUMMARY

The strongest recommendations concern methods and directly inspectable mismatches:
observation models, common evaluation targets, equivalence, and development/test
separation. Physical recommendations are less certain because the relevant actuator
studies use different designs, small cohorts, or different measured endpoints.
Cross-domain prognostics and selective-prediction papers justify experiments, not
claims of success. No located source validates this project's generator, proves
global novelty, or shows that a more complex model will resolve Study C.
